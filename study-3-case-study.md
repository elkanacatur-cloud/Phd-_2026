## 3.5 Study 3: In-depth single case study

Study 3 is the explanatory centre of gravity of this thesis. Where Study 1 read across a body of literature to assemble sensitising concepts, and Study 2 worked across institutions to develop themes about how temporal misalignment is experienced, Study 3 moves inside a single case to reconstruct the mechanism itself. The unit of analysis is the temporal misalignment between electoral cycles and project timelines, traced across more than one electoral cycle so that the mechanism can be observed doing its work rather than inferred from a single point in time.

### 3.5.1 Rationale for the design

The research question for Study 3 asks how and why the misalignment between electoral cycles and project timelines operates, not how often it appears across a population of projects. A question of that shape calls for a design that can hold a phenomenon and its context together and reason from one to the other, which is the defining feature of case study research (Yin, 2018). This study adopts a critical realist stance. On this view social reality is stratified, so the generative mechanism behind the observed misalignment operates at a level deeper than the events it produces and is real without being open to direct observation, reachable only by reconstructing the events through which it works; knowledge of the case is correspondingly treated as theory-laden without being theory-determined, with prior theory guiding the reconstruction while remaining open to correction by it (Fletcher, 2017). The temporal ordering of a project therefore cannot be separated from the institutional arrangements and electoral rhythms in which it unfolds, and access to the mechanism depends on the depth of a single reconstructed case rather than on breadth of coverage. The claim the study pursues is an analytic generalisation to a theoretical explanation of temporal misalignment, not a statistical generalisation to a wider population of projects (Martinsuo & Huemann, 2021a; Yin, 2018).

Case selection is purposive and theoretical rather than convenient (Seawright & Gerring, 2008). A case qualifies against three explicit criteria:

- a delivery timeline spanning at least two electoral cycles;
- involvement of more than one level of government;
- sufficient documentary trace and field access to sustain an in-depth reconstruction [ISI: kriteria akses dan ketersediaan data spesifik].

The first criterion is decisive: it lets the mechanism be traced across a change of elected administration rather than within a single term.

The boundary of the case is drawn explicitly. Inside the case sit the project and the sequence of decisions through which it was planned, funded, revised, and delivered; the surrounding electoral, institutional, and fiscal arrangements are treated as context that bears on the case without being part of it (Martinsuo & Huemann, 2021a; Yin, 2018). The case is bounded in time by [ISI: rentang waktu yang dicakup, mencakup sekurang-kurangnya dua electoral cycles]. Within this boundary the study uses embedded units of analysis, namely [ISI: misalnya fase-fase proyek atau organisasi pemerintahan yang berbeda], each examined in its own right before the case is read as a whole. A single case is chosen over a multiple-case design because the object of interest is the unfolding of one mechanism over time, and tracing that unfolding closely across successive electoral cycles demands the analytical depth that a single, well-bounded case affords rather than the comparative breadth that a multiple-case design would buy at the cost of that depth (Martinsuo & Huemann, 2021a; Yin, 2018).

Study 3 does work that neither of the earlier studies could. The integrative review established what the field already treats as salient but could not show a mechanism at work in a concrete setting, and the cross-institutional interviews of Study 2 established recurrent themes across institutions but held the account at the level of actors' reported experience rather than the sequence of decisions that produced the misalignment. The themes carried forward from Study 2, together with the sensitising concepts from Study 1, enter Study 3 as initial propositions that the analysis may support, elaborate, or reject rather than as conclusions awaiting confirmation, which is how critical realist inquiry treats the prior theory it begins from (Fletcher, 2017). The design has a known limitation in that a single case supports theoretical rather than empirical transferability, and this study addresses that limitation directly through thick contextual description of the case and its setting and through transparency about the reasoning that connects evidence to claims, so that readers can judge for themselves how far the explanation travels to settings they know (Martinsuo & Huemann, 2021b).

### 3.5.2 Data collection

The case is reconstructed from several complementary kinds of evidence rather than from any single source, because a mechanism that plays out over years leaves different kinds of trace that only converge when read together (Yin, 2018). Each kind of source does distinct work against the unit of analysis: documents supply the dated record of what was decided and when, locating project decisions against the electoral calendar, while interviews supply the reasoning of the actors who made those decisions and the considerations that the record does not preserve. Drawing on more than one kind of source serves to build a stronger explanation and to test how well it holds when confronted with independent evidence, not to verify a single authoritative version of events; where sources diverge, the divergence is itself analytical material about how the case is remembered and recorded rather than a discrepancy to be smoothed away (Martinsuo & Huemann, 2021b).

Documentary and archival data form the backbone of the temporal reconstruction and include planning documents, budget documents, audit reports, and other related documents. The span of documents collected is set to open before the first relevant electoral cycle and to close after the later one, so that the record brackets more than one change of elected administration and the decisions on either side of each transition are visible. From these documents the study assembles a dated chronology of the case, which becomes the spine of the temporal analysis by fixing each project decision in relation to the electoral calendar. Documents are read critically as artefacts produced for institutional purposes, so a budget paper, an audit report, or a planning document is treated as an institutional claim made in a specific setting rather than as a neutral record of what occurred (Martinsuo & Huemann, 2021b).

Interviews within the case are conducted with informants selected for their position in the decisions under study, on the basis of their involvement in the governance process during the period of misalignment. These interviews differ from those of Study 2 in both focus and instrument: they concentrate on the concrete sequence of decisions inside this one case rather than on cross-institutional experience, and they use the documentary chronology as an elicitation device, putting dated decisions to informants so that their accounts attach to specific moments in the record.

Interviews are conducted in the language the informant chooses, whether English or another language, and are transcribed in the original language, with translation reserved for the reporting stage so that analysis works on informants' own words (Martinsuo & Huemann, 2021b). Because the interviews are retrospective, they carry known risks of faulty recall and of sensemaking imposed after the fact, and the study handles this by reading interview claims against the documentary trace and treating points where recollection and record diverge as material to be explained rather than as a version to be accepted or discarded (Martinsuo & Huemann, 2021b; Yin, 2018).

Ethical approval for the research will be sought from the University of Leeds before any fieldwork begins. Once approval is granted, access will be negotiated with the case organisation and relevant project stakeholders to agree arrangements for participation, interviews, and documentary access. Any restrictions encountered during fieldwork will be reported explicitly and their implications for the analysis made clear. Given the limited number of individuals occupying the positions of interest, confidentiality requires particular care. Role descriptions are therefore presented at an appropriate level of generality, and identifying details are omitted where necessary.

Data are stored in a case study database that separates source material from analytical notes, allowing the chain of evidence from data to interpretation to be traced (Yin, 2018). The database provides a systematic record of interviews, documents, and field notes and their use during analysis. This audit trail supports transparency in how the findings are developed and presented.

### 3.5.3 Data analysis

The analysis works at the level of the mechanism and reasons abductively, beginning from the empirical patterns of the case while drawing on prior theory as background rather than as a hypothesis to be tested, redescribing those patterns through candidate theoretical concepts and moving back and forth between evidence and theory to arrive at the account that most plausibly explains them, while holding to the critical realist commitment to identifying the generative mechanism that underlies the observed misalignment (Conaty, 2021; Fletcher, 2017; Martinsuo & Huemann, 2021b). The first analytical step assembles the case chronology, positioning each project decision relative to the electoral cycles it fell between, so that the temporal ordering the explanation must eventually account for is laid out before any explanation is attempted. This orientation differs from the reflexive thematic analysis of Study 2 in both aim and assumption: Study 2 develops themes as interpretive constructions of patterned meaning across accounts, whereas Study 3 reconstructs a temporal mechanism within a single bounded case and works towards the most plausible explanation of why its sequence unfolded as it did. The two approaches are kept distinct rather than blended: the analysis here is theory-engaged and researcher-driven in the critical realist sense rather than inductively data-driven, and the distinction is maintained throughout the analysis (Fletcher, 2017).

The propositions carried forward from Study 1 and Study 2 are stated as predicted patterns before they are set against the empirical pattern of the case, so that what the propositions lead the study to expect is fixed in advance of the comparison (Yin, 2018). Correspondence and non-correspondence between predicted and observed patterns are judged on logical and argumentative grounds, by weighing how fully a proposition accounts for the sequence in the record, and not by any statistical test or mechanical matching. Non-correspondence is treated as information that forces a proposition to be revised rather than as an inconvenient finding to be set aside, and rival explanations are formulated explicitly and tested against the same body of evidence, so that the analysis stays open to alternative accounts while ruling out those that prove less plausible, and the account the study reaches has been made to compete for the case rather than assumed into it (Conaty, 2021; Seawright & Gerring, 2008; Yin, 2018).

Explanation building proceeds iteratively: the study frames an initial explanation of the mechanism, confronts it with the evidence, revises it where the evidence resists, and confronts the revised explanation with the next body of evidence (Yin, 2018). This iteration is disciplined and documented, with each revision and its warrant recorded as the analysis proceeds, so that a genuine revision of the explanation can be distinguished from a post hoc adjustment made to fit the data. The explanation the study settles on has to account for the temporal ordering of the case in full, including the events that the initial explanation did not anticipate, rather than only the stretches that fit. Pattern matching and explanation building are joined in this: the first shows where the initial propositions hold and where they fail against the case, and the second constructs an explanation that accommodates both the holding and the failing.

Quality in the analysis rests on maintaining a traceable chain of evidence from evidence to claim, drawing on more than one kind of source, testing explicit rival explanations, keeping analytic decisions transparent, and discussing the developing account with supervisors (Yin, 2018). A reflexive journal records how the explanation shifts across the analysis, so that the path from initial to final account is itself part of the record rather than reconstructed afterwards. The researcher's own background in project management within government is treated as an interpretive resource to be interrogated rather than a neutral vantage point, in the sense that the prior experience an abductive case study draws on has at the same time to be recognised and disciplined through the researcher's reflexive self-awareness (Conaty, 2021), and in particular the analysis stays alert to an initial disposition to read the permanent administrative organisation more sympathetically than the elected actors whose electoral cycles bear on the project, since that disposition could quietly shape which explanation looks most plausible. Coding is carried out both by hand and in NVivo, with manual coding used to stay close to the chronology and the software used to organise evidence and hold the chain of evidence together across a large and multilingual corpus.
