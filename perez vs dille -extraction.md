# Entrainment & Temporal Fit/Misfit — Separate Extraction from Two Papers

> Extraction only. The two papers are treated strictly separately; no comparison
> or synthesis is made here. All quotations are verbatim from the source MD files.
> Page numbers are the printed journal page numbers shown in each document.
>
> - **Paper 1:** Pérez-Nordtvedt, Payne, Short & Kedia (2008), "An Entrainment-Based
>   Model of Temporal Organizational Fit, Misfit, and Performance," *Organization
>   Science* 19(5), pp. 785–801.
> - **Paper 2:** Dille & Söderlund (2011), "Managing inter-institutional projects:
>   The significance of isochronism, timing norms and temporal misfits,"
>   *International Journal of Project Management* 29, pp. 480–490.

## Paper 1 — Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. (2008)

### 1. DEFINITION

**"Entrainment"** — defined explicitly, and twice (a general/"traditional"
definition and the authors' own organizational one):

- General/traditional (abstract, p. 785; restated in Introduction, p. 785):
  > "Entrainment refers to the synchronization of the tempo and/or phase of two or
  > more activities within a system." (abstract, p. 785)
  > "Entrainment is traditionally defined as the synchronization of the tempo
  > and/or phase of two or more activities within a system (Ancona and Chong 1996,
  > Aschoff 1979, McGrath et al. 1984, Standifer and Bluedorn 2006)." (p. 785)

- *Social entrainment*, quoted from a cited source (p. 786):
  > "They define social entrainment as the 'capturing and modification of human
  > activity cycles by various social customs, norms, and institutions' (McGrath
  > and Rotchford 1983, p. 78)."

- The authors' own **organizational entrainment** definition (p. 788):
  > "organizational entrainment is defined here as the processes by which
  > organizations cope with temporal change by synchronizing (i.e., tempo matching
  > and/or phase aligning) their endogenous cyclic activities to those of the
  > external environment (i.e., the zeitgeber)."

**"Temporal fit/misfit"** — *temporal fit* is defined explicitly; *temporal misfit*
is characterized relationally rather than given a single one-line definition:

- Temporal (O-E) fit (abstract, p. 785):
  > "the concept of organization-environment (O-E) temporal fit, which is a state
  > of synchronization or alignment of organization and environment activity
  > cycles."

- Temporal fit has a **tolerance range** (p. 792):
  > "the argument asserted here is that temporal fit has a tolerance range. As
  > such, as long as the organization's activity cycles fit the temporal
  > environment within a certain degree, the organization will maintain satisficing
  > levels of performance."

- Temporal *misfit* — characterized via consequences and via the four quadrant
  states rather than a standalone definitional sentence:
  > "temporal misfit implies inefficiencies, substandard performance, and the
  > potential death of the organization over time." (abstract, p. 785)
  The operational forms are phase misfit, tempo misfit, and "full temporal misfit"
  (Figure 3, Quadrants II–IV, p. 791): "Organizations can be in a state of phase
  misfit... in tempo misfit... or in both tempo and phase misfit with the temporal
  environment (Quadrant (IV): Full temporal misfit)." (p. 791)

### 2. ORIGIN

The paper gives a multi-stage origin for entrainment:

- **Physics first, then biology** (p. 785):
  > "The concept of entrainment, first developed in physics and then widely used in
  > biology, holds the potential for organizational scholars to continue this
  > borrowing process and enrich the field of organization science (Ancona and
  > Chong 1996, Bluedorn 1993)."
- **Named originator — Huygens** (p. 786):
  > "The phenomenon of synchronization or 'entrainment of frequency' was first noted
  > by Christian Huygens, a Dutch physicist of the 17th century."
- **Into the social sciences** (p. 786):
  > "Entrainment was introduced into the social sciences by Hall (1983) and McGrath
  > and Rotchford (1983)."
  With social entrainment developed by McGrath et al. (1984); *zeitgeber* / circadian
  lineage attributed to Aschoff (1979) and Pittendrigh (1972); into organization
  science via Ancona & Chong (1996) and Bluedorn (1993).

For **temporal fit/misfit**, the fit/misfit lineage is traced to the strategic-
management/contingency literature — e.g., "Venkatraman and Camillus 1984" and the
SARFIT model (Donaldson 1987, 1999) (pp. 790, 792) — combined with entrainment
theory.

### 3. POSITION

The paper's stance is to move entrainment from metaphor to an explicit
organization-level model. It argues prior work only "mentioned entrainment's
theoretical potential at the organization level, rather than outlining a specific
research agenda" (p. 787), and fills that gap with a conceptual model and eight
propositions linking **organization–environment (O-E) temporal fit/misfit to
performance and survival**. Its central claim is that temporal misfit (in tempo
and/or phase) causes inefficiency, substandard performance, and potential
organizational death, and that organizations respond by **entraining**
(synchronizing to the environmental zeitgeber) or by **temporal enactment**
(imposing their own cycles so the environment entrains to them) — with a tolerance
range that makes fit a matter of degree, not a dichotomy.

### 4. EVIDENCE LOCATION

- **p. 785 (Abstract & Introduction):** traditional definition of entrainment;
  tempo/phase; O-E temporal fit definition; physics→biology origin.
- **p. 786 (section "Entrainment in the Social Sciences"):** Huygens origin; Hall
  (1983) & McGrath & Rotchford (1983); social-entrainment definition; zeitgeber;
  McGrath et al. (1984) assumptions; tempo vs. phase entrainment.
- **p. 787 (Figure 1; "Organizational Entrainment"):** cycles/tempo/phase/magnitude;
  tempo misfit → phase misfit; the "specific research agenda" gap statement.
- **p. 787–788 (Key Assumptions):** five assumptions.
- **p. 788–789 (Definition and Types):** organizational-entrainment definition;
  phase vs. tempo entrainment; entrainment vs. adaptation; vs. pseudoentrainment.
- **p. 789–791 (the model; Figures 2–3):** O-E temporal fit/misfit quadrants;
  entrain vs. temporal enactment.
- **p. 790–791 ("Temporal Fit, Misfit, and Organizational Performance"):**
  fit/misfit lineage (Venkatraman & Camillus; Burton et al.; SARFIT).
- **p. 792–793 (Propositions 1–4):** tolerance range; deviation from optimal fit.
- **p. 793–795 (Propositions 5–8):** entrainment/enactment; degree & length of
  misfit; intraentrainment.
- **p. 796–798 (Discussion & Future Research):** entrainment and contingency /
  institutional / population-ecology theories; "isochronism."

### Related terms / spellings noted (Pérez-Nordtvedt et al.)

**"temporal entrainment," "social entrainment," "organizational entrainment,"
"tempo entrainment," "phase entrainment,"** plus coined variants **"intraentrainment,"
"extraentrainment," "pseudoentrainment,"** and the contrasting mechanism **"temporal
enactment."** Fit vocabulary: **"O-E temporal fit," "temporal misfit," "phase misfit,"
"tempo misfit," "full temporal misfit," "tolerance range."** Core terms: **tempo,
phase, cycle, zeitgeber, pacer, rhythm.** Note: this paper coins the term **"isochronism"**
only in passing on p. 796 (as an institutional-theory extension); it is *not* one of
its named entrainment types.

## Paper 2 — Dille & Söderlund (2011)

### 1. DEFINITION

**"Entrainment"** — the paper **does not give a standalone definition of the term
"entrainment."** It never states "entrainment is defined as...". Instead it invokes
"entrainment theory" and defines only the two *sub-types* it borrows, and does so
through a citation:

> "It is also claimed that organizations which set their activity cycles to those
> of their external environment (i.e. phase entrainment) or adjust the pace of
> their activity cycles to match those of the external environment (i.e. tempo
> entrainment) will perform better (Khavul et al., 2010:106)." (p. 485)

Elsewhere "entrainment theory" / "entrainment theorists" are named and attributed to
Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. (2008) (pp. 485, 488, Table 2), but the umbrella concept
"entrainment" is used without its own explicit definition — plainly stated here so
as not to invent one.

**"Temporal fit/misfit"** — defined explicitly, both in the running text and in
Table 2, and grounded in Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. (2008):

- In text (section 7, "Temporal fit/misfits," pp. 485–486):
  > "the suggested idea and use of the notion of temporal fit/misfit concern to what
  > extent phases, i.e., timing of activities, and/or tempo (speed) of activities
  > are aligned among the actors involved (Pérez-Nordtvedt et al., 2008)."

- Table 2 (p. 488), **Temporal fit**:
  > "Institutionally aligned perceptions of time and timing. Temporal fit implies
  > that phase (timing of activities) and/or tempo (speed) of activities are
  > synchronized due to institutional matching."
  (Theoretical influence listed: "Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. (2008).")

- Table 2 (p. 488), **Temporal misfit**:
  > "Institutionally diverging timing norms that cause collaboration and
  > synchronization problems. Temporal misfit implies that phase (timing of
  > activities) and/or tempo (speed) of activities differ due to diverging
  > institutional requirements."
  (Theoretical influences listed: "Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. (2008); Dahlgren and
  Söderlund (2001).")

Related core concept — **isochronism** (Table 2, p. 488), where entrainment theory
enters:
> "Organizations sharing the same environment tend to adopt the same time
> orientation and over time become temporally aligned in terms of when things should
> be done and how fast things should be done (phase and speed)."
(Theoretical influences listed: "Institutional theory: e.g., DiMaggio and Powell
(1983); Erlingsdóttir and Lindberg (2005)" **and** "Entrainment theory: e.g.,
Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. (2008).")

### 2. ORIGIN

- For **entrainment** and its **phase/tempo** sub-types, the paper takes the concept
  second-hand — no physics/biology/Huygens lineage is given. It attributes phase and
  tempo entrainment to **Khavul et al. (2010:106)** (p. 485) and "entrainment theory"
  / "entrainment theorists" to **Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. (2008)** (pp. 485, 488).
  The idea of adopting "the same time" is cited to **"(Pérez-Nordtvedt et al.,
  2008)"** (p. 484), and the dominant-macro-cycle / zeitgeber idea to "entrainment
  theorists (Pérez-Nordtvedt et al., 2008)" (p. 485).
- For **temporal fit/misfit**, the origin stated is **Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. (2008)**
  (text p. 485–486 and Table 2), with temporal misfit additionally citing **Dahlgren
  and Söderlund (2001)** (Table 2, p. 488).
- **Isochronism** is presented as the paper's own extension, built on institutional
  theory — "isomorphism" (DiMaggio and Powell, 1983), alongside "isopraxism" and
  "isonymism" (Erlingsdóttir and Lindberg, 2005) — combined with entrainment theory.

### 3. POSITION

Entrainment is **not** the paper's headline concept; its three core concepts are
**isochronism, timing norms, and temporal fit/misfit** (abstract, p. 480; section 4,
p. 484). Entrainment theory is imported chiefly to underpin **isochronism** — the
homogenization process by which organizations in a field "come to resemble each other
in the tempos and phases of their activity cycles" (p. 484) via dominant macro
cycles/zeitgebers — and to supply the **temporal fit/misfit** vocabulary, which the
authors then **recast in institutional terms** (fit = "institutional matching";
misfit = "diverging institutional requirements," Table 2, p. 488). The paper's own
claim is that in "inter-institutional projects," actors carry conflicting timing
norms from different institutional fields, and these produce **temporal misfits** that
damage cooperation and coordination (propositions 1–5, pp. 486–487).

### 4. EVIDENCE LOCATION

- **p. 480 (Abstract):** names "temporal fit/misfit" as one of three core concepts.
- **p. 484 (section 5, "Isochronism"):** isochronism as "adopting 'the same time'
  (Pérez-Nordtvedt et al., 2008)"; isomorphism/isopraxism/isonymism lineage.
- **p. 485 (section 5 continued):** phase entrainment and tempo entrainment glossed
  via "(Khavul et al., 2010:106)"; dominant macro cycles as "zeitgebers" attributed
  to "entrainment theorists (Pérez-Nordtvedt et al., 2008)"; Pérez-Nordtvedt et al.
  (2008:796) quotation on "timing or synchronization of activity cycles."
- **p. 485–487 (section 7, "Temporal fit/misfits"; section 8, Discussion):** temporal
  fit/misfit definition grounded in Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. (2008); "timing legitimacy";
  hypotheses on conflicting timing norms causing misfit.
- **p. 488 (Table 2):** explicit definitions of isochronism, timing norm, temporal
  fit, and temporal misfit, each with theoretical influences (incl. "Entrainment
  theory: Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. (2008)").
- **p. 486–487 (Conclusions & Propositions 1–5):** isochronism → timing norms →
  temporal misfits chain.
- **p. 490 (References):** Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. (2008); Khavul et al. (2010);
  Dahlgren and Söderlund (2001).

### Related terms / spellings noted (Dille & Söderlund)

Uses **"phase entrainment"** and **"tempo entrainment"** (borrowed sub-types) and refers
to **"entrainment theory"** / **"entrainment theorists,"** but never **"social entrainment,"
"organizational entrainment,"** or a standalone "entrainment" definition. Its own coined
/ adopted vocabulary sits in an **institutional** register: **"isochronism"** (its central
time concept), alongside **"isomorphism," "isopraxism," "isonymism,"** plus **"timing
norms," "macro pacers," "dominant macro cycles," "zeitgebers," "global time," "timing
legitimacy."** Fit vocabulary: **"temporal fit," "temporal misfit(s)"** (frequently
pluralized, e.g., "temporal misfits"). Note the term **"isochronism"** is this paper's
own emphasis; in Paper 1 the same word appears only once and in passing.
