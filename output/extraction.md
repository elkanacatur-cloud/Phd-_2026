## Step 0 — Locator convention

I inspected each file for surviving page markers. In all three, page markers
**survive** as running heads/footers, so the convention used is **`p. N`** for
every file. One caveat applies to all three files and shapes how I assign pages:

> **In these conversions the running-head/footer page marker is rendered at the
> _foot_ of the page it labels, not the top.** A claim is therefore assigned to
> the page whose marker is the next one _following_ the claim's text. This is
> reliable for text sitting well inside a page, but a claim that falls exactly on
> a column break or page break can be misassigned by one page. Such
> boundary-straddling claims are flagged `NEEDS PAGE FROM PRINT COPY` and

## Step 2 / 2b — Per-source extraction

# Source A — Braun & Clarke (2021), "One size fits all?" (Qual. Res. Psychol. 18(3): 328–352)

**Terminology.** Uses "thematic analysis (TA)" as an umbrella term and
"**reflexive TA**" for its own approach; also names "**coding reliability**" TA
and "**codebook**" TA as the other two clusters (p. 333). Explicitly
distinguishes the labels: "TA refers not to a singular approach, but rather to a
cluster of sometimes conflicting approaches" (p. 333). TA is called an "umbrella
term for, or a 'fuzzy' set … of, approaches" (p. 335).

**What the method is claimed to do.** All TA approaches "aim to identify and make
sense of patterns of meaning across a dataset" (p. 331). Reflexive TA offers "a
distinct way of working with qualitative data" (p. 329) in which themes are the
analytic *outcome* of coding and theme development, capable of "deeply
interpretative, theorised analyses" (p. 339), not merely descriptive.

**Philosophical commitments.** Stated as a **requirement with a boundary**:
reflexive TA is theoretically flexible "albeit constrained by qualitative
paradigmatic and epistemological assumptions about meaningful knowledge" (p.
331). It is positioned within the **Big Q** qualitative paradigm (Kidder & Fine's
small q / Big Q distinction, p. 329). Coding-reliability/positivist quality logic
is declared **incompatible**: demonstrating coding reliability and avoiding
"bias" is "illogical, incoherent and ultimately meaningless" in reflexive TA (p.
334). Researchers should always "specify the philosophical and theoretical
assumptions informing their use of TA, even inductive TA" (p. 337).

**Position on realism.** Addressed. Reflexive TA is "suited to both experiential
(e.g. critical realist, contextualist) and critical (e.g. relativist,
constructionist) framings of language, data and meaning" (p. 331). The paper
argues *against* positioning TA "as a realist or essentialist method that simply
retrieves truth and reality" (p. 338, Problem five) — i.e. realist use is
possible but must not be the *assumed default*. Critical-realist use is named as
a compatible **experiential** framing only; no claim is made about causal or
generative mechanisms.

**Procedure.** "Most recent articulation" of the six phases (p. 331):
(1) data familiarisation and writing familiarisation notes; (2) systematic data
coding; (3) generating initial themes from coded and collated data; (4)
developing and reviewing themes; (5) refining, defining and naming themes; (6)
writing the report. The phases are "not intended to be followed rigidly" and
"blend together" as craft skill develops (p. 331).

**Role of the researcher.** Subjectivity is "an analytic resource" (p. 330) and
"a resource for knowledge production, which inevitably sculpts the knowledge
produced" (p. 334), not a threat to contain. Coding is "open and organic, with no
use of any coding framework" (p. 334). Coding is **interpretation, not
discovery**: themes "must be analytic outputs, not inputs" (p. 342); researchers
should "always avoid claiming that themes emerged" (p. 343).

**What the source rejects** (the ten problems, pp. 333–343): (1) assuming TA is
one approach; (2) "citing without reading" — attributing to the authors
practices they reject (p. 336); (3) unjustified/incompatible "mash-ups", e.g.
reflexive TA with codebooks and coding-reliability measures (p. 336); (4)
assuming TA is atheoretical — "TA can never be conducted in a theoretical vacuum"
(p. 337); (5) assuming TA is only realist/essentialist or
experiential/phenomenological (p. 338); (6) assuming TA is only descriptive —
"Interpretative depth lies in the skill of the analyst, not the method" (p. 340);
(7) confusing codes and themes — codes capture "(usually just) one facet",
themes are "multi-faceted" (p. 340); (8) confusing themes and topics/domain
summaries (pp. 340–341); (9) "emerging themes" language (pp. 342–343); (10)
"uncritical acceptance of what we say" / proceduralism (p. 343). Also explicitly
rejects, as things wrongly attributed to them: consensus coding, measuring coding
reliability, developing themes before coding, data/theme saturation, theme-
frequency counting, constant comparative analysis (p. 336).

**Provenance of key constructs.**
- **Reflexive TA** — `Primary` (Braun & Clarke; the paper's own approach, p. 331).
- **Tripartite typology (coding reliability / codebook / reflexive)** — `Primary`;
  cited to their own "Braun et al. 2019a" (p. 333).
- **Big Q / small q** — `Borrowed` from Kidder and Fine (1987) (p. 329).
- **"Confused q"** — `Borrowed`; attributed in-text to co-author Nikki Hayfield
  (p. 329).
- **Themes as patterns of shared meaning united by a central concept** —
  `Primary`; cited to Braun & Clarke (2013); Braun et al. (2014) (p. 341).
- **Domain summary / topic-as-theme critique** — `Primary` (their prior work);
  "analytic foreclosure" is `Borrowed` from Connelly and Peltzer (2016) (p. 340).
- **"Diamonds scattered in the sand"** — `Borrowed`/self-quote, Braun & Clarke
  (2016), p. 740 (cited p. 342); **"thematic discovery"** — `Borrowed` from
  Guest, Bunce and Johnson (2006: 66) (p. 342).
- **"Themes reside in our heads"** — `Borrowed` from Ely et al. (1997: 205–6),
  quoted p. 343.
- **Methodolatry** — `Borrowed` from Chamberlain (2000); **proceduralism** —
  `Borrowed` from King and Brooks (2017) (p. 330).
- **Subjectivity as resource** — `Primary` framing, supported via Gough & Madill;
  in this paper cited generally (see Source B, p. 590, for explicit Gough &
  Madill 2012 attribution).
- **"Fuzzy set"** — `Borrowed` from Madill and Gough (2008) (p. 335).
- **Boyatzis "bridge the divide" / coding reliability** — `Relay`/`Borrowed`,
  attributed to Boyatzis (1998) (pp. 333–334).

### Step 2b — Explanatory summary (Source A)

The paper responds to a concrete problem in the authors' own framing: a
manuscript reviewer told them to "discuss how they attempted to avoid bias in
their analytic process" (p. 328), a demand they treat as symptomatic of a wider
failure to recognise that "thematic analysis" names not one method but several
paradigmatically divergent ones. Their target is the assumption that a single
quality standard — above all "coding reliability" — applies to all TA.

The argument runs through Kidder and Fine's distinction between small q
(qualitative technique inside a positivist paradigm) and Big Q (qualitative
technique inside a qualitative paradigm) (p. 329). Because different TA
"versions" sit in different paradigms, quality criteria that make sense for one
are incoherent for another. They cluster TA into coding reliability, codebook and
reflexive variants (p. 333), then diagnose ten recurring "problems" in papers
that cite them — from importing grounded-theory saturation and inter-rater
reliability into reflexive TA (pp. 336–337), to presenting topic summaries as
themes (pp. 340–341), to the phrase "themes emerged" (pp. 342–343). What turns on
the distinctions is practical: whether coding precedes themes or produces them,
whether multiple coders and consensus are quality markers or category errors, and
whether a "theme" is a shared-meaning pattern or a bucket for a topic. They close
with a twenty-question evaluation tool for reviewers and editors (Table 1, pp.
345–346).

What the paper *argues* is that quality is paradigm-relative and that reflexive TA
must be judged on qualitative-paradigm terms. What it *assumes*, and does not
defend here, is the value framework of Big Q qualitative research itself — that
subjectivity is a resource, that meaning is situated and contextual, and that
"knowingness" is the mark of good scholarship; these are treated as settled.

What it does not tell the reader: it gives no worked, step-by-step demonstration
of reflexive TA on data (it points elsewhere); it does not resolve *how much* a
priori theoretical steering is compatible with reflexive TA before the analysis
becomes "codebook"; it says nothing about whether themes can support causal or
generative claims; and its argument is situated in psychology and adjacent
qualitative fields — it does not establish that its diagnoses transfer to
disciplines with different reporting conventions.

# Source B — Braun & Clarke (2019), "Reflecting on reflexive thematic analysis" (Qual. Res. Sport Exerc. Health 11(4): 589–597)

**Terminology.** Introduces the authors' preferred label "**reflexive TA**" (p.
589; title). Retains "TA" as the general term and names the three clusters
"**coding reliability TA, codebook TA and reflexive TA**" (p. 592, Table 1).
Distinguishes labels explicitly and revises their own phase terminology from
"searching for themes" to "**generating (initial) themes**" (p. 592, Table 1).

**What the method is claimed to do.** Reflexive TA produces themes that are
"analytic outputs developed through and from the creative labour of … coding" (p.
593); themes are "creative and interpretive stories about the data" (p. 593).

**Philosophical commitments.** Stated as a **shift toward a requirement**. In
2006 they called TA "theoretically flexible"; now they hold TA is flexible "only
as a generic method", while "specific iterations of TA encode particular
paradigmatic and epistemological assumptions" (p. 591). Reflexive TA "procedures
reflect the values of a qualitative paradigm, centring researcher subjectivity"
(p. 593, Table 1). Coding-reliability approaches are tied to a (post-)positivist
paradigm (p. 593).

**Position on realism.** Addressed **only** via the keyword list and the
post-positivism contrast. Keywords include "post-positivism" and "epistemology;
ontology; paradigm" (p. 589). Coding reliability TA is described as
"positivist-oriented (small q)" (p. 593). No statement is made here about
realist, critical-realist or causal use of reflexive TA specifically. `Not
addressed` for realist/critical-realist use of *reflexive* TA.

**Procedure.** This commentary does not re-list all six phases; it revises one
phase label — "we now prefer the term 'generating (initial) themes'" over
"searching for themes" (p. 592) — and describes coding as requiring "a continual
bending back on oneself" (p. 593). `Not addressed` as a full phase list (by
design; the paper points to their other writing).

**Role of the researcher.** Central. "The researcher's role in knowledge
production is at the heart of our approach!" (p. 593). Subjectivity is "a
resource (see Gough and Madill 2012), rather than a potential threat" (p. 590).
Coding is **interpretation/creation, not discovery**: "Themes do not passively
emerge from either data or coding; they are not 'in' the data" (p. 593). Quality
reflexive TA is "not about following procedures 'correctly'" or "'accurate' and
'reliable' coding" (p. 593). If multiple researchers code, it is "collaborative
and reflexive … rather than seeking a consensus on meaning" (p. 593).

**What the source rejects.** Rejects: treating TA as one approach; "untheorised
mashups" of their approach with grounded-theory techniques and/or coding-
reliability measures (p. 589); confusing "domain summaries" with themes (p. 592);
presenting TA as "a rigid, linear series of stages" or forcing "either/or"
choices between semantic *or* latent, inductive *or* deductive (p. 592); the
Boyatzis (1998) attempt to "bridge the divide" with positivism via coding
reliability (p. 590); and the "themes emerged" account.

**Provenance of key constructs.**
- **Reflexive TA** — `Primary` (p. 589).
- **Tripartite typology (coding reliability / codebook / reflexive)** — `Primary`;
  cited to Braun et al. (2018) (p. 592).
- **Central organizing concept / themes as shared meaning** — `Primary`; cited to
  Braun & Clarke (2013); Braun, Clarke & Rance (2014) (p. 592).
- **Domain summary themes** — `Primary`; cited to Clarke and Braun (2018);
  "underdeveloped themes" `Borrowed` from Connelly and Peltzer (2016) (p. 592).
- **Big Q / small q** — `Borrowed` from Kidder and Fine (1987) (p. 593).
- **Subjectivity as resource** — `Borrowed`/supported, Gough and Madill (2012)
  (p. 590).
- **Methodolatry** — `Borrowed`, Chamberlain (2000); **proceduralism** —
  `Borrowed`, King and Brooks (2017a) (p. 589).
- **Framework analysis / template analysis → "codebook TA"** — `Borrowed`
  originators: Ritchie and Spencer (1994); King and Brooks (2017b) (p. 593).
- **Coding reliability / "bridge the divide"** — `Relay`/`Borrowed`, Boyatzis
  (1998); also Guest, MacQueen & Namey (2012) (pp. 590, 593).
- **"Recipe vs. adventure" framing / "knowingness"** — `Primary` term
  ("knowingness"), paraphrasing Willig (2008) (`Borrowed`) (pp. 590–591).
- **Commentary inspired by** Mauthner and Doucet (2003) — `Borrowed` (p. 589).

### Step 2b — Explanatory summary (Source B)

The problem this commentary responds to, in the authors' framing, is their own
success: the 2006 paper became "one of the most cited academic papers of recent
decades" (p. 590), and with that reach came systematic misreading. They argue
that when they wrote in 2006 they "assumed our paper would only be of interest to
a fairly small audience of people who 'got it'" (p. 590) and so left unstated the
qualitative-paradigm assumptions behind the method. The commentary's purpose is
to surface those assumptions and, in doing so, to rename the approach "reflexive
TA."

The reasoning is autobiographical and conceptual. They trace their commitments to
doctoral training at Loughborough in critical, constructionist and feminist
qualitative psychology (pp. 589–590), which taught them to prize "knowingness" —
research treated as a deliberative process involving explicit ontological and
epistemological decisions (p. 590). Against that background they reject Boyatzis's
attempt to "bridge the divide" between positivist and interpretive research using
coding-reliability measures (p. 590). The pivotal move is a revised claim about
flexibility: TA is theoretically flexible "only as a generic method", while each
specific iteration encodes paradigmatic assumptions (p. 591). What turns on this
is that reflexive TA is no longer offered as paradigm-neutral; it belongs to the
qualitative (Big Q) paradigm, and its distinctive commitments — subjectivity as
resource, organic coding, themes as constructed "stories" (p. 593) — follow from
that. They also distinguish themes (patterns of shared meaning around a central
organizing concept) from domain summaries, and relabel phase three "generating
initial themes" to kill the "themes emerge" metaphor (p. 592).

What the paper *argues* is the reconceptualisation of flexibility and the
centrality of reflexivity. What it *assumes*, without defending here, is that the
qualitative paradigm's values are the correct frame for their method and that
subjectivity is a resource rather than a bias.

What it does not tell the reader: it gives no procedural how-to (it repeatedly
defers to their other publications); it does not say how reflexive TA handles
realist or causal questions; it does not specify where the boundary lies between
a "welcome" theoretically-knowing mash-up and an incoherent one; and its examples
and framing are drawn from sport-and-exercise and psychology, so transfer to
other disciplines is asserted only in passing ("and beyond", p. 594).

**Terminology.** Primary label used is "**thematic analysis (TA)**" (p. 1). The
term "**reflexive thematic analysis**" appears when discussing researcher
subjectivity — noting confusion "particularly with respect to reflexive thematic
analysis, where researcher subjectivity is embraced rather than minimized" (p.
1). The paper does **not** operate the coding-reliability / codebook / reflexive
tripartite typology; it treats TA largely as a single six-phase framework. It
does **not** distinguish those labels systematically (`Not addressed` as a
typology).

**What the method is claimed to do.** TA offers "a structured yet flexible
framework for identifying, analyzing, and interpreting patterns of meaning" (p.
1); it enables "both descriptive and rich conceptual analysis across diverse
topics" (p. 1).

**Philosophical commitments.** Stated as a **possibility / lack of constraint**,
not a requirement. "Thematic analysis is not bound to any specific theoretical
framework, making it a versatile tool suitable for both inductive and deductive
approaches" [ref 5] (p. 1). TA is "ideal for researchers aiming to identify
patterns of meaning without being constrained by specific epistemological
commitments" (p. 1). Table 1 lists TA's "Philosophical foundation" as
"Theoretically flexible (realist or constructivist)" (p. 2; damaged table).

**Position on realism.** Addressed **only** in the comparison table: TA's
philosophical foundation is given as "realist or constructivist" and its
flexibility as "High – across disciplines and paradigms" (Table 1, p. 2; damaged
table). No qualification is offered restricting realist use. Critical realism /
post-positivism are **not** discussed in the body; ref [9] (a critical-realist,
causal-explanation TA paper) appears only in the reference list, not the text.
`Not addressed` for critical-realist or post-positivist use in prose.

**Procedure.** Braun & Clarke's six phases, each given a full section (pp. 1–5):
(1) familiarization with the data; (2) generating initial codes; (3) searching
for themes; (4) reviewing themes; (5) defining and naming themes; (6) writing the
report. Note: uses the **original** "searching for themes" label for phase 3
(p. 3), not the revised "generating initial themes." A 16-item checklist (Table
4) operationalises the six phases (p. 5).

**Role of the researcher.** Reflexivity is emphasised throughout as documenting
"personal assumptions, biases, and preconceptions … in a reflexive journal" (p.
2). Coding is called "both a technical and interpretative act" (p. 2). The paper

interpretative process" and cautions against treating themes as "pre-existing
entities that merely 'emerge'" [ref 12] (p. 4) — a discovery-vs-interpretation
stance aligned with Braun & Clarke, but see provenance flag below.

**What the source rejects.** Identifies as errors/misuse: presenting themes as
"'emerging' … without acknowledging their role in constructing them" (p. 1);
conflating "TA with simple coding" (p. 1); overcoding and undercoding (p. 3);
using direct quotations as theme names (p. 4); and giving "the impression of

### Step 2b — Explanatory summary (Source C)

The problem this paper responds to, in its own framing, is practical confusion
among applied (largely health-science) researchers: TA is widely used but
"there remains confusion surrounding its application, particularly with respect
to reflexive thematic analysis" (p. 1), reporting is inconsistent across fields,
and novices "conflate TA with simple coding" (p. 1). Its stated aim is corrective
and pedagogical — to give "a detailed description of the six steps involved in
thematic analysis" and a "16-item checklist to ensure adherence" (p. 1).

The paper's argument is largely expository rather than polemical. It walks
through Braun & Clarke's six phases one at a time (familiarization, initial
coding, searching for themes, reviewing themes, defining/naming themes, writing
up), giving for each its purpose, key activities, reflexive considerations,
challenges and significance (pp. 1–5, condensed in Tables 3–4). Around this it
places TA against grounded theory, IPA and content analysis (Table 1, p. 2),
arguing TA's selling point is theoretical flexibility and accessibility. The
reasoning that recurs is that rigor comes from systematic, documented,
reflexive practice — reflexive journaling, transparent coding decisions, and a
checklist — rather than from statistical or reliability machinery. It repeatedly
endorses the "themes are constructed, not emergent" position (pp. 1, 4, 5).

What the paper *argues* is that following the six phases carefully, with
reflexivity and a checklist, yields rigorous TA. What it *assumes*, and does not
defend, is that TA can be presented as essentially one method with one procedure,
and that it is epistemologically unconstrained — "not bound to any specific
theoretical framework" (p. 1). It also assumes the six-phase framework can be
compressed into a compliance checklist without distortion.

What it does not tell the reader: it does not distinguish reflexive TA from
codebook or coding-reliability TA (the distinction that Sources A and B treat as
central); it does not state the qualitative-paradigm boundary that Braun & Clarke
now place on flexibility; it uses the superseded phase-3 label "searching for
themes" without noting the revision; it says nothing about causal or generative
claims; and — a provenance concern for the researcher — it attributes several
