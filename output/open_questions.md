# Open Questions

Things the three supplied sources do **not** settle, written as questions the
researcher would need to resolve from other material (the published PDFs, the
authors' other writing, or additional literature). Nothing here is inferred as an
answer; each item marks a genuine gap or an unverifiable detail.

## Substantive gaps in the sources

1. **Can TA themes support causal or generative-mechanism claims?** None of the
   three sources addresses this. Ahmed (2025) lists Fryer (2022) — a critical-
   realist "producing causal explanations" TA paper — only in its reference list
   [ref 9], never in the body. To answer, the researcher must go to Fryer (2022)
   or other critical-realist TA sources directly.

2. **Where exactly is the line between reflexive TA's "theory-as-lens" deductive
   use and a codebook approach?** Braun & Clarke (2021) permit deductive,
   theory-driven coding (p. 331) yet forbid predetermined theme-buckets and
   codebooks (pp. 332, 342). They do not specify how much a priori structuring is
   tolerable before the work stops being reflexive TA. Unresolved by these
   sources.

3. **Under what conditions are a priori categories acceptable alongside inductive
   coding in *applied* research?** Ahmed (2025) endorses "inductive and deductive"
   use (p. 1) but states no conditions; Braun & Clarke give a lens-vs-frame
   principle but no operational threshold. The applied-health reader is left
   without a decision rule.

4. **Which paradigm does Ahmed et al. (2025) actually commit to?** The paper's
   prose is reflexive-leaning ("themes are actively constructed", p. 4) but its
   Table 1 ("realist or constructivist", p. 2), its reliability/reproducibility
   language, and its 16-item compliance checklist point toward a codebook/
   coding-reliability sensibility. The paper does not locate itself in the
   tripartite typology, so its paradigmatic stance is unsettled.

5. **Does Braun & Clarke's diagnosis transfer beyond psychology / sport-and-
   exercise?** Both B&C papers are situated in those fields (2019 p. 594; 2021
   throughout). Whether the "ten problems" and "twenty questions" apply to other
   disciplines is asserted ("and beyond") but not demonstrated. Needs
   discipline-specific evidence.

6. **Full reflexive-TA procedure at operational detail.** B&C (2019) defers the
   phase-by-phase how-to to other publications; B&C (2021) gives only the
   six-phase headings (p. 331). A researcher wanting the worked procedure must
   consult Braun & Clarke (2013), Braun et al. (2019a), Terry et al. (2017), or
   the authors' later textbook — none supplied here.

## Citation-provenance items to verify (governance-critical)

7. **Ahmed et al. attributes Braun-&-Clarke positions to third-party sources.**
   In prose the paper says "Braun and Clarke caution against treating themes as
   pre-existing entities that merely 'emerge'" but cites **Christou (2022)** [ref
   12] (p. 4); a related claim cites **De Paoli (2023)** [ref 18] (p. 5). Before
   citing Ahmed for any Braun-&-Clarke claim, verify against the primary Braun &
   Clarke sources; do not treat Ahmed as a primary locator for those positions.

8. **Ahmed's generic "Braun and Clarke" citations often point to their 2024
   paper [ref 8], not the 2006 original [ref 1].** Several method claims tagged
   "Braun and Clarke" carry [ref 8] = Braun & Clarke (2024, *Palliative
   Medicine*, RTARG). If the researcher needs the 2006 origin, the mapping must
   be checked reference-by-reference in the print copy.

9. **Is the 16-item checklist (Ahmed Table 4) original to Ahmed et al.?** No
   external source is credited for the checklist itself; it is tagged `Primary`
   (Ahmed et al.) on that basis. Confirm from the print copy that it is not
   reproduced/adapted from another author.
