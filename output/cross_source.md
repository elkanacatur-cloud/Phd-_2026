# Cross-Source Answers (Step 3)

Answered only from the Step 2 extractions. Every element carries source + page.
Sources: **A** = Braun & Clarke (2021); **B** = Braun & Clarke (2019); **C** =
Ahmed et al. (2025).

---

## Q1. Paradigm-bound or paradigm-flexible? (and any qualification)

The two Braun & Clarke papers and the Ahmed paper give **different answers**.

- **B (2019):** Reflexive TA is paradigm-**flexible only in a qualified sense**.
  TA is theoretically flexible "only as a generic method"; specific iterations
  "encode particular paradigmatic and epistemological assumptions" (B, p. 591).
  Reflexive TA specifically "procedures reflect the values of a qualitative
  paradigm" (B, p. 593, Table 1). Qualification: the flexibility is *within* the
  qualitative (Big Q) paradigm; it is not paradigm-neutral.

- **A (2021):** Same qualified position, stated more sharply. Reflexive TA can be
  done "with quite different guiding theories (albeit constrained by qualitative
  paradigmatic and epistemological assumptions …)" (A, p. 331). The precise
  qualification: it is "suited to both experiential (e.g. critical realist,
  contextualist) and critical (e.g. relativist, constructionist) framings" (A,
  p. 331) — i.e. flexible *across* qualitative epistemologies — but
  coding-reliability/positivist quality logic is "illogical, incoherent and
  ultimately meaningless" within it (A, p. 334). So: flexible inside the
  qualitative paradigm, **incompatible** with the positivist one.

- **C (2025):** Presents TA as paradigm-**flexible without the qualification**.
  TA "is not bound to any specific theoretical framework" (C, p. 1) and lets
  researchers identify patterns "without being constrained by specific
  epistemological commitments" (C, p. 1); Table 1 rates its flexibility "High –
  across disciplines and paradigms" (C, p. 2). C does **not** restrict reflexive
  TA to the qualitative paradigm and does not state the Big Q boundary.

**Net:** A and B treat reflexive TA as paradigm-flexible *but bounded by the
qualitative paradigm*; C treats TA as paradigm-flexible *and essentially
unbounded*. This is a genuine disagreement (see Q5).

## Q2. What distinguishes reflexive TA from codebook / coding-reliability approaches?

Distinguishing features, drawn from A (Problem one, pp. 333–335) and B (pp. 592–593):

**Coding-reliability TA** (A, p. 333):
1. Neopositivist; core concern with "'objective' and 'unbiased' coding" (A, p. 333).
2. Uses a **codebook / coding frame** for coding (A, p. 333).
3. Uses **multiple independent coders** (A, p. 334).
4. Uses **inter-rater reliability / consensus** as a quality measure (A, pp. 333–334).
5. **Themes developed early or before analysis**; often deductive (A, p. 333).

**Codebook TA** (A, pp. 333, 335; B, p. 593):
6. Sits "within a qualitative paradigm (albeit with some pragmatic compromises)"
   (A, p. 333).
7. Uses a **structured coding framework** (template/matrix/framework) (A, p. 335).
8. **Early theme development** (of some or all themes) (A, p. 335).
9. But **consensus and inter-rater reliability are *not* quality measures** (A,
   p. 333).
10. Examples named: framework analysis, matrix analysis, template analysis (A,
    p. 335; B, p. 593).

**Reflexive TA** (A, pp. 334, 342; B, p. 593):
11. "Fully embrace[s] qualitative research values" and researcher subjective
    skill (A, p. 334).
12. "A research team is not required or even desirable" (A, p. 334); if used,
    coding is collaborative, "rather than seeking a consensus on meaning" (B,
    p. 593).
13. **No coding framework** — "Coding is open and organic, with no use of any
    coding framework" (A, p. 334).
14. **Themes are the final analytic outcome**, developed through coding — "Themes
    should be the final 'outcome' of data coding" (A, p. 334); "analytic outputs,
    not inputs" (A, p. 342).
15. Quality is **not** "'accurate' and 'reliable' coding" but reflexive,
    thoughtful engagement (B, p. 593).
16. Subjectivity is a **resource**, not a threat to contain (A, p. 334; B, p. 590).

**C (2025):** Does **not** draw this distinction. It treats TA as one six-phase
framework and only notes in passing that reflexive TA "embraces rather than
minimize[s]" subjectivity (C, p. 1). The codebook / coding-reliability categories
are `Not addressed` in C.

## Q3. Can themes support causal / generative-mechanism claims (vs. patterned-meaning claims)?

- **A (2021):** `Not addressed` as a causal-mechanism question. A frames themes
  as "patterns of shared meaning, united by a central concept" (A, p. 341) and
  argues for interpretative depth (A, p. 340), but makes **no** claim that themes
  can warrant causal or generative-mechanism explanations. It names
  "critical realist, contextualist" as a compatible *experiential framing* (A,
  p. 331) without extending this to causal claims.

- **B (2019):** `Not addressed`. Themes are "creative and interpretive stories
  about the data" (B, p. 593); no causal/generative-mechanism discussion.

- **C (2025):** `Not addressed` in the body. A critical-realist, causal-
  explanation TA paper (Fryer 2022) appears **only in C's reference list** [ref
  9], cited for the point that "TA lacks formal procedures for sampling,
  comparison, and saturation" (C, p. 1) — not for any causal-mechanism claim.

**Net:** None of the three supplied sources addresses whether TA themes can
support causal or generative-mechanism claims. The nearest trace is C's
uncited-in-body reference [9] (Fryer 2022) and A's mention of critical-realist
framings (A, p. 331), neither of which makes a causal-mechanism claim.

## Q4. A priori categories alongside inductive coding — addressed? under what conditions acceptable?

- **A (2021):** Addressed, with conditions. Analysis "can be more inductive or
  more theoretical/deductive … a continuum, rather than dichotomy" (A, p. 331).
  Deductive use is acceptable when existing theory serves as "the lens through
  which we analyse and interpret data" (A, p. 331) — e.g. attachment theory or
  Foucault's theory of sexual ethics (A, p. 332). **Condition/limit:** even
  inductive TA "can never be conducted in a theoretical vacuum" (A, p. 337); but
  a priori categories as a **fixed coding frame/codebook** are *not* reflexive TA
  — "coding is not … a process for finding evidence for pre-conceptualised
  themes" and "themes … must be analytic outputs, not inputs" (A, pp. 332, 342).
  So: a priori *theory-as-lens* is acceptable; a priori *coding categories /
  predetermined themes* pushes the work into codebook/coding-reliability TA.

- **B (2019):** Addressed lightly. Endorses "a mix of semantic *and* latent,
  inductive *and* deductive" rather than either/or (B, p. 592). No further
  conditions specified; full procedure deferred to other writing. Consistent with
  A: deductive orientation is fine, predetermined theme-buckets are not reflexive
  TA (B, p. 593).

- **C (2025):** Notes TA is "suitable for both inductive and deductive
  approaches" [ref 5] (C, p. 1) but **states no conditions** governing the use of
  a priori categories alongside inductive coding. `Not addressed` as to
  conditions.

## Q5. Where do the sources disagree?

1. **Scope of flexibility / paradigm boundary (C vs A & B).** C: TA "is not bound
   to any specific theoretical framework" and is usable "without being
   constrained by specific epistemological commitments" (C, p. 1; Table 1 "High –
   across disciplines and paradigms", p. 2). A & B: flexibility is *bounded* —
   flexible "only as a generic method" (B, p. 591), "constrained by qualitative
   paradigmatic and epistemological assumptions" (A, p. 331). Direct
   contradiction on whether TA is epistemologically unconstrained.

2. **One method vs. plural cluster (C vs A & B).** C presents TA as essentially
   one six-phase framework with a compliance checklist (C, pp. 1–5, Table 4). A's
   first and organising "problem" is "assuming TA is one approach" (A, p. 333);
   B likewise lists "treating TA as one approach" as poor practice (B, p. 589).

3. **Reliability / rigor language (C vs A & B).** C ties rigor to a "16-item
   checklist" and speaks of "rigor and reliability" and "reproducibility" (C,
   pp. 1, and abstract). A argues that demonstrating coding reliability and
   avoiding "bias" is "illogical, incoherent and ultimately meaningless" in
   reflexive TA (A, p. 334) and lists checklists/consensus/reliability among
   things wrongly attributed to them (A, p. 336). Tension between C's checklist-
   rigor framing and A/B's rejection of reliability criteria for reflexive TA.

4. **Phase-3 label (C vs B & A).** C uses the original "searching for themes"
   (C, p. 3). B explicitly revised this to "generating (initial) themes" to stop
   the "themes emerge" reading (B, p. 592), and A lists phase 3 as "generating
   initial themes" (A, pp. 331, 343). C lags the authors' own revision.

5. **Realist framing without qualification (C vs A).** C's Table 1 gives TA's
   foundation as "realist or constructivist" with no caveat (C, p. 2). A devotes
   Problem five to warning against positioning TA "as a realist or essentialist
   method that simply retrieves truth and reality" (A, p. 338).

**Between A and B:** No substantive disagreement found. B (2019) sets out the
reconceptualised flexibility and the reflexive label; A (2021) builds on it with
the ten problems and the twenty-question tool. They are consistent, A being the
later and more operational statement.

**Internal tension within C (not a between-source disagreement, but noted):** C's
prose endorses the reflexive "themes are constructed, not emergent" position (C,
pp. 1, 4, 5) while its Table 1 and checklist embody a more realist/procedural
framing, and it attributes several "Braun and Clarke" positions to third-party
secondary sources (Christou 2022 [ref 12], De Paoli 2023 [ref 18]) rather than to
Braun & Clarke directly (C, pp. 4–5). See `open_questions.md`.
