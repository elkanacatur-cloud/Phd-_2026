## 3.3 Research Strategies

The empirical work answers two linked questions: how institutional contexts shape the misalignment between electoral cycles and project timelines, and how and why that misalignment operates. No single strategy answers both, so the study uses two qualitative strategies in sequence: a cross-institutional interview study that works across institutions, and a case study that works within one project. This division fits the study's critical realism, which looks for a mechanism both as a pattern across contexts and in the way it works inside a single context (Fletcher, 2017). Study 1, an integrative review, is not an empirical strategy but the conceptual foundation for both. It develops the sensitising concepts that orient the two strategies and indicate where temporal misalignment is likely to be found.

The interview study examines how actors in different institutions experience and respond to the misalignment between electoral cycles and the project timeline. This sits in the reasons and interpretations actors hold, which documents do not record, so it can be reached only by asking actors directly (Dunwoodie et al., 2022). The strategy produces initial propositions about the mechanisms at work, which the case study then takes up. Its limit is that breadth across institutions cannot show how a mechanism works through one concrete sequence of decisions over time.

The case study explains how and why the mechanisms work within their institutional and temporal setting. A case study suits this task because it gives an in-depth view of a phenomenon in its specific context (Martinsuo & Huemann, 2021). It also follows the misalignment across a chronology of decisions that spans more than one electoral cycle, which the interview study cannot do. Martinsuo and Huemann (2021) noted that a phenomenon which evolves over time is best studied by following the process as it unfolds. The two strategies connect in sequence: the propositions from the interview study enter the case study as starting points to be developed and tested further, not as findings to be confirmed. Where the case departs from them, the difference is analytic material for a fuller explanation. The relationship is cumulative, and the case study is its explanatory centre. The sections that follow present each study in turn, starting with Study 1.
