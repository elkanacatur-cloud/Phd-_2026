## Time Horizon

A study's time horizon classifies its design by how the data are collected rather than by how long the phenomenon under study lasts, separating a single period of collection from repeated collection that follows the same units as they change (Saunders et al., 2023). This research comprises three studies, and although Study 2 and Study 3 will be conducted in different research periods, neither will observe the same participants, institutions, or projects on more than one occasion. Analysing a process that unfolds across time is not the same as adopting a longitudinal design, since the chronology of governance decisions will be reconstructed retrospectively, after the events have occurred, rather than observed in real time at successive points. Because it collects from each actor, institution, and project only once, the design is classified as cross-sectional, also termed a snapshot (Saunders et al., 2023; Flick, 2014).

The object of this research is temporal, since it concerns the misalignment between electoral cycles and project timelines. The design reaches this object through documentary sources that span successive electoral cycles and through informants who held office under different administrations, which together show how one governance decision conditioned the next. The next section sets out how each of the three studies, an integrative review, a set of cross-institutional interviews, and an in-depth case study, contributes to this account.
