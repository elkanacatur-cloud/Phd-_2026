## Time Horizon

This research adopts a cross-sectional time horizon, which both Saunders et al. (2023) and Flick (2014) also describe as a snapshot: it examines its phenomena as they stand within a bounded period of primary data collection rather than tracking the same units repeatedly as they change. The primary data will be gathered within a window of roughly four months, so the design incorporates no repeated measurement of the same informants, institutions, or documents at successive intervals. The finite duration of a doctoral programme is one practical consideration behind this decision, but it is not the governing one, and the design does not rest on convenience. The decisive reason lies in the research question, which calls for an explanation of the generative mechanisms that link electoral cycles to project governance rather than a measured trajectory of change, and such mechanisms can be reconstructed analytically without observing a case in real time throughout its full course.

The object of this research is temporal, since it concerns the misalignment between electoral cycles and project timelines, and this fairly raises whether a design that is not longitudinal can capture it. Following the logic of a retrospective study (Flick, 2014), the design reconstructs this process rather than measuring it, tracing the sequence of governance decisions through documentary sources that span successive electoral cycles and through informants who held office under different administrations to show how one decision conditioned the next. The next section sets out how each of the three studies, an integrative review, a set of cross-institutional interviews, and an in-depth case study, contributes to this account.
