# Entrainment — Comparison of Two Papers

> **Ancona & Chong (1992)** — "Timing Is Everything," *AoM Best Papers Proceedings*
> pp. 166–169 — and **Pérez-Nordtvedt, Payne, Short & Kedia (2008)** — "An
> Entrainment-Based Model...," *Organization Science* 19(5), pp. 785–801.

## A) Comparison Table

| | **Ancona & Chong (1992)** | **Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. (2008)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Two definitions. Borrowed biological one: "a process where a biological or behavioral rhythm is modified by external cycles or pacers" whose "periodicities and phases... are modified not their occurrences" (p. 166). Their own coined org-theory one: "the adjustment or moderation of one behavior to synchronize or to be in rhythm with another" (p. 166). | Two definitions. General/"traditional" one: "the synchronization of the tempo and/or phase of two or more activities within a system" (p. 785). Their own coined one: "organizational entrainment... the processes by which organizations cope with temporal change by synchronizing (i.e., tempo matching and/or phase aligning) their endogenous cyclic activities to those of the external environment (i.e., the zeitgeber)" (p. 788). |
| **Stated origin** | **Biology only.** "This paper applies the biological concept of entrainment to organization theory" (p. 166). Cited lineage: McGrath, Kelly & Machatka (1986) and Aschoff (1979, for *zeitgebers*). No physics / no Huygens. | **Physics → biology → social sciences → organization science.** "first developed in physics and then widely used in biology" (p. 785); "first noted by Christian Huygens, a Dutch physicist of the 17th century" (p. 786); "introduced into the social sciences by Hall (1983) and McGrath and Rotchford (1983)" (p. 786); into org science via Ancona & Chong (1996) and Bluedorn (1993). |
| **Position / role in the paper** | Entrainment as "a new theoretical lens" (p. 166) to bring *time* into organization theory. Extends the deterministic biological notion to add human agency ("beyond this determinism to incorporate some degree of voluntarism," p. 166). Builds a **three-type typology** — tempo, synchronic, harmonic — and proposes hypotheses linking entrainment to performance. | Entrainment moved from metaphor to an explicit **organization-level model with eight propositions** on **O-E temporal fit/misfit → performance/survival**. Central mechanism pair: **entrain** vs. **temporal enactment**; introduces a **tolerance range** (fit as degree, not dichotomy). Positioned as complementary to contingency, institutional, and population-ecology theories. |

## B) Differences

### B.1 — Differences the papers themselves acknowledge

Because Ancona & Chong (1992) predates the 2008 paper, only Pérez-Nordtvedt et al.
can engage across the pair; the engagement is **one-directional**.

- **Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. explicitly build on Ancona & Chong** (citing the 1996
  edition). The extraction records that their Figure 1 is "an adaptation and
  extension of Ancona and Chong (1996)" and that they adopt Ancona & Chong's
  *tempo* entrainment. So the later paper openly frames itself as continuing the
  earlier work rather than disputing it.

- **Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. openly frame the gap they fill against prior work
  (including Ancona-type contributions).** Per the extraction, they state prior
  research only "mentioned entrainment's theoretical potential at the organization
  level, rather than outlining a specific research agenda" (p. 787). This is an
  acknowledged difference in ambition: earlier work supplies a *lens*, theirs
  supplies an *explicit model and propositions*.

- **Each paper openly frames its own role differently** (self-declared, not hidden):
  Ancona & Chong announce a "new theoretical lens" (p. 166); Pérez-Nordtvedt et al.
  announce a conceptual model with eight testable propositions. Both statements are
  on the record in the extraction.

### B.2 — Differences an outside reader would see (neither paper names)

- **Origin story divergence.** Ancona & Chong state the concept is biological
  ("the biological concept of entrainment," p. 166) with no mention of physics.
  Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. root it in physics first (Huygens, p. 786) before biology.
  Neither paper flags that its origin account differs from the other's.

- **The "traditional definition" attribution smooths over Ancona's actual wording.**
  Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. attribute the tempo/phase definition partly to "Ancona and
  Chong 1996" (p. 785), but the extraction shows Ancona & Chong's *own coined*
  definition is worded quite differently — "the adjustment or moderation of one
  **behavior** to synchronize or to be in rhythm with another" (p. 166), not a
  tempo/phase-of-cycles formula. An outside reader notices the later paper folds
  Ancona's behavior-based wording into a cycle-based "traditional" formula; neither
  paper remarks on the shift.

- **Terminology mismatch for the phase concept.** Ancona & Chong's second type is
  "**synchronic** entrainment" (pace + rhythmic phase); Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. use
  "**phase** entrainment." The extraction flags these as the closest counterparts,
  but neither paper reconciles the labels.

- **Typology has no clean one-to-one mapping.** Ancona & Chong give three types
  (tempo, synchronic, **harmonic**); Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. give two (tempo, phase)
  plus coined variants (**intraentrainment, extraentrainment, pseudoentrainment**)
  and a contrasting mechanism (**temporal enactment**). Ancona's *harmonic*
  entrainment — where "the two processes need not have the same pace or rhythm"
  (p. 166) — has no counterpart in the 2008 scheme, and the 2008 coinages have none
  in the 1992 scheme. Neither paper notes the non-correspondence.

- **Determinism vs. voluntarism, weighted oppositely.** Ancona & Chong foreground
  agency, explicitly pushing "beyond this determinism to incorporate some degree of
  voluntarism" (p. 166). Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. include a "strategic choice"
  assumption yet, per the extraction, present their overall model as "largely
  deterministic." An outside reader sees the emphasis reversed; neither paper
  contrasts its stance with the other's.

## C) Open questions for synthesis

*(Surfaced, not resolved. "Incompatible" = the two would give conflicting answers to
the same question; "different emphasis" = compatible content, different weighting.)*

**Points that look genuinely incompatible (as worded in the extraction):**

1. **Scope of what counts as entrainment — behavior vs. cyclic tempo/phase.**
   Ancona & Chong's coined definition covers "one **behavior**" adjusting to be "in
   rhythm with another" (p. 166) and their *harmonic* type explicitly allows
   processes that "need not have the same pace or rhythm." Pérez-Nordtvedt et al.
   define entrainment as synchronization "of the **tempo and/or phase**" of "cyclic"
   / "endogenous cyclic activities" (pp. 785, 788). A case that qualifies under
   Ancona's harmonic entrainment (no shared pace or rhythm) appears to fall *outside*
   the 2008 definition. These do not merely differ in emphasis; they draw the
   category boundary in different places.

2. **Stated origin discipline — biology vs. physics.** Ancona & Chong locate the
   concept's source in biology; Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. locate its first development
   in physics (Huygens). As bare origin claims these conflict, though they share
   biology as a common node — worth deciding whether this is a true contradiction or
   two compatible slices of one lineage.

**Points that look like differences in emphasis only:**

3. **Determinism vs. voluntarism/strategic choice.** Both admit agency (Ancona's
   "voluntarism"; Pérez's "strategic choice" assumption), but weight it differently
   (Pérez's model is "largely deterministic"). Same ingredients, different balance.

4. **Lens vs. model.** "New theoretical lens" (Ancona) vs. "explicit model +
   propositions" (Pérez) is a difference in level of formalization/ambition, not a
   conflict in what entrainment *is*.

5. **Typology labels and granularity.** tempo/synchronic/harmonic vs.
   tempo/phase(+intra-/extra-/pseudo-entrainment, temporal enactment). Overlapping
   where both name *tempo*; the mismatch is coverage and vocabulary, not
   contradiction — except where it feeds question 1 (harmonic having no 2008 home).
