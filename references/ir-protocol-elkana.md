## Overarching Research Question

How does misalignment between electoral cycles and megaproject timelines shape megaproject governance?

## Research Question

**RQ1:** How is temporal misalignment conceptualised in a megaproject context?

## Background and Rationale

This study adopts an Integrative Review (IR) approach to synthesise and critically evaluate existing knowledge on temporal misalignment in megaprojects. IRs are suited to concept development and theory building in fragmented and interdisciplinary fields (Cronin & George, 2023; Torraco, 2016). By integrating conceptual, empirical, and theoretical contributions from diverse sources, the review examines how temporal misalignment has been conceptualised and situates these insights within the megaproject context (Kutcher & LeBaron, 2022; Torraco, 2016). The IR also permits the inclusion of relevant grey literature, such as government reports and audits, which helps mitigate publication bias and capture temporal dynamics in megaproject practice (Kutcher & LeBaron, 2022; Oermann & Knafl, 2021).

## Methodology

### Scoping and Conceptual Structuring

The search strategy began with a scoping exercise to identify foundational and highly cited sources recognised for their theoretical contributions to studies of temporality and megaprojects. These key publications were used for keyword extraction, allowing the researcher to identify how core concepts and phenomena are labelled and discussed by leading scholars (Booth & Papaioannou, 2016; Bramer et al., 2018). The extracted keywords were organised into three analytically distinct search blocks: (1) Temporality (construct), (2) Conflict or misalignment (phenomenon), and (3) Megaprojects (context). This structure balances precision (relevance of retrieved studies) and recall (comprehensiveness of coverage) while maintaining a clear conceptual logic linking construct, phenomenon, and empirical setting.

### Search Strategy

A preliminary exploratory search was conducted to identify foundational literature on temporality in megaproject contexts. This supported conceptual familiarisation and informed the selection and refinement of keywords used in the structured search (Snyder, 2019).

- **Block 1 — Temporality.** Keywords drawn from foundational works on temporal organisation: tempo, temporality, temporal, pacing, rhythm, time reckoning, temporal orientation, entrainment (Geiger et al., 2021; Omidvar et al., 2025; Pérez-Nordtvedt et al., 2008; Stjerne et al., 2019). Search string: `(temporal* OR pacing OR tempo* OR r*ythm* OR "time reckoning" OR "temporal orientation" OR entrain*)`. Truncation retrieves grammatical and conceptual variants; `r*ythm*` captures standard and variant spellings.
- **Block 2 — Conflict and Misalignment (phenomenon).** Keywords from studies on inter-organisational temporal dynamics: temporal misalignment, temporal misfit, asynchrony, temporal disruption, temporal conflict, temporal tension, temporal miscoupling (Blagoev et al., 2024; Casillas & Huising, 2025; Dille et al., 2023; Dille & Söderlund, 2011; Reinecke & Ansari, 2015). Search string: `(misfit* OR misalignment* OR asynchron* OR disruption* OR conflict* OR tension* OR miscoupling*)`. Anchored to Block 1 with a Boolean AND, retrieving compound constructs such as temporal misfit and temporal tension.
- **Block 3 — Megaprojects (context).** Built on the validated keyword list of Denicol et al. (2020). After a pilot search, `infrastructure*` was removed because it retrieved a large volume of irrelevant results (e.g., colonisation and workforce issues) rather than megaproject development. This evidence-based refinement is consistent with the iterative nature of IR methodology (Snyder, 2019; Torraco, 2016). The string was extended, drawing on Brookes et al. (2017) and Dille and Söderlund (2011), to incorporate inter-organisational and institutional terms that retrieve studies conceptualising megaprojects as inter-institutional systems.

The three blocks were operationalised with Boolean operators. Consistent with an IR approach, emphasis was placed on conceptual coverage and theoretical sensitivity rather than exhaustive reporting of database-specific Boolean syntax.

### Database and Source Selection

The review draws on peer-reviewed journal articles identified through Scopus and Web of Science, selected in combination for comprehensive coverage across the disciplines this topic requires.

### Inclusion Logic

Inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied across two dimensions, technical and conceptual, consistent with the iterative logic of IR methodology (Cronin & George, 2023).

**Technical criteria** (initial screening): publication type, language, and disciplinary scope. Literature from unrelated fields (medical, biological, physical sciences) was excluded, as temporality there is operationalised as a physical or biological variable rather than a socially constructed phenomenon (Tranfield et al., 2003). Searches were restricted to peer-reviewed journal articles published in English. Peer-review ensures a consistent level of rigour and transparency (Briner et al., 2009). The English-language restriction reflects English as the predominant language of academic publishing in the fields covered, minimising the risk of excluding influential contributions while supporting consistent interpretation (Stockemer & Wigginton, 2019).

**Conceptual criteria:** five sequential screening questions (SQ1–SQ5) applied to titles and abstracts, each a one-way filter. Records excluded at any stage were removed permanently; flagged studies were retained for full-text assessment without presuming inclusion (Kutcher & LeBaron, 2022).

- **SQ1 (Temporality):** Does the paper engage with temporality as a social or institutional construct, rather than treating time solely as a physical, biological, or computational variable?
- **SQ2 (Misalignment):** Does the paper address misalignment, tension, conflict, or disruption between temporal systems?
- **SQ3 (Context):** Is the empirical or analytical context related to projects, megaprojects, inter-organisational collaboration, or the governance of long-duration undertakings?
- **SQ4 (Conceptual contribution):** Does the paper offer a conceptualisation, theorisation, or empirical examination of temporal dynamics, rather than focusing purely on descriptive scheduling or planning techniques?
- **SQ5 (Transferability):** Does the core argument demonstrate relevance to megaprojects or long-duration project settings?

SQ2 and SQ4 operate jointly but distinctly: SQ2 identifies temporal friction or disruption; SQ4 assesses whether the engagement advances conceptual understanding rather than remaining descriptive. SQ5 retains studies from inter-organisational, programme-governance, or long-duration settings where the argument is analytically transferable to megaprojects.

### Screening Result (Pre-Screening)

The search was conducted across Scopus and Web of Science, yielding 814 and 1,279 records respectively (2,093 total). Technical criteria excluded 188 records from Scopus and 415 from Web of Science. After removing duplicates, a pre-screening corpus of 481 records was established. Conceptual screening (SQ1–SQ5) against titles and abstracts then retained 77 sources for full-text assessment.

### Data Extraction and Synthesis Approach

Data extraction links the screened literature to conceptual synthesis. The review adopts an inclusive extraction approach that prioritises how temporality and temporal misalignment are interpreted and theorised, rather than aggregating findings (Snyder, 2019; Toronto & Remington, 2020). The IR includes theoretical contributions, which distinguishes it from conventional systematic reviews; such articles develop, analyse, or extend conceptual frameworks and cannot be appraised using design-based quality criteria applied to empirical studies (Toronto & Remington, 2020).

Drawing on a concept-based screening logic similar to Watton (2023), sources are retained if they meet at least one of two conditions: (1) they offer an explicit conceptualisation of temporality or temporal structures in relation to megaprojects, or (2) they examine forms of temporal tension or misalignment analytically transferable across megaproject or megaproject-like contexts.

### Analytical Framework

Thematic synthesis is under consideration as an analytical option for structuring and integrating insights across the literature (Thomas & Harden, 2008). At this stage it is an exploratory option rather than a fixed framework. Consistent with the research question's focus, synthesis is expected to follow an inductive, question-driven logic (Cronin & George, 2023; Omidvar et al., 2025).
