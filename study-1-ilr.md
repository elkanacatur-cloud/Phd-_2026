# Study 1 — Integrative Literature Review

## 3.3 Method

### 3.3.1 Rationale of Design

Study 1 employs an integrative literature review (ILR) to address RQ1. Torraco (2016) described the ILR as a form of research that reviews, critiques, and synthesises representative literature on a topic so that new perspectives on that topic emerge. These perspectives arise inductively from the review rather than from a framework fixed at the outset, which makes the method concept-driven and suited to building understanding of a construct (Cronin & George, 2023). The ILR also integrates diverse sources, holding empirical, theoretical, and conceptual work together in one synthesis rather than confining the review to a single type of evidence (Cronin & George, 2023). This orientation separates the ILR from the systematic literature review. A systematic review answers a narrow, well-specified question by aggregating comparable studies, and it fits mature topics and standardised interventions where the evidence base is homogeneous. The ILR instead fits emerging topics and concept development, where the literature is dispersed and the aim is to clarify how a construct is understood rather than to estimate an effect (Torraco, 2016).

Temporal misalignment meets the conditions that call for an ILR. The construct is still emerging, and its literature is fragmented across project studies, organisational studies, and political science, with each field naming and theorising temporal phenomena in its own terms. An ILR draws these dispersed accounts together and examines how temporal misalignment is conceptualised across them, which a review confined to one discipline or one method could not achieve. The method also admits relevant grey literature, such as government reports and audits, which supplies empirical detail on how temporal misalignment appears in megaproject practice (Kutcher & LeBaron, 2022). This design fits the abductive logic set out in the preceding sections. The review develops the conceptual foundation for the research programme, and Studies 2 and 3 test and refine that foundation against empirical cases.

The output of Study 1 is a set of sensitising concepts rather than a conceptual framework. Sensitising concepts direct the inquiry by indicating what to look for, while leaving their precise content open to development through later empirical work. This framing suits the emerging state of the literature and the abductive design, and it avoids overpromising a settled framework that the current evidence does not support. The sensitising concepts produced here guide the design, data generation, and analysis of Studies 2 and 3.
