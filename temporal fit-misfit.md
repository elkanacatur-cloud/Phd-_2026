## A) Comparison Table

| | **Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. (2008)** | **Dille & Söderlund (2011)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | *Entrainment* defined explicitly and multiply: traditional — "the synchronization of the tempo and/or phase of two or more activities within a system" (p. 785); own — "organizational entrainment is defined here as the processes by which organizations cope with temporal change by synchronizing... their endogenous cyclic activities to those of the external environment (i.e., the zeitgeber)" (p. 788). *Temporal fit* — "a state of synchronization or alignment of organization and environment activity cycles" (p. 785), with a "tolerance range" (p. 792). *Misfit* characterized relationally (phase/tempo/full misfit, Fig. 3, p. 791). | *Entrainment* — **no standalone definition**; only the sub-types glossed via citation: "phase entrainment" and "tempo entrainment" per "(Khavul et al., 2010:106)" (p. 485). *Temporal fit* — "Institutionally aligned perceptions of time and timing. Temporal fit implies that phase... and/or tempo... are synchronized due to institutional matching" (Table 2, p. 488). *Misfit* — "Institutionally diverging timing norms... phase... and/or tempo... differ due to diverging institutional requirements" (Table 2, p. 488). |
| **Stated origin** | Multi-stage: "first developed in physics and then widely used in biology" (p. 785); "first noted by Christian Huygens, a Dutch physicist of the 17th century" (p. 786); "introduced into the social sciences by Hall (1983) and McGrath and Rotchford (1983)" (p. 786); into org science via Ancona & Chong (1996), Bluedorn (1993). Fit is graded via a tolerance range, not a dichotomy. | Entrainment is **not** the headline; the three core concepts are **isochronism, timing norms, and temporal fit/misfit** (abstract p. 480). ## B) Differences

### B.1 — Differences the papers themselves acknowledge

Because Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. (2008) predates Dille & Söderlund (2011), only the
later paper can engage across the pair; the engagement is **one-directional**.

- **Dille & Söderlund explicitly build on Pérez-Nordtvedt et al.** Per the extraction,
  they name "Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. (2008)" as the source of "entrainment theory" and
  of the temporal fit/misfit construct, both in the running text (pp. 485–486) and in
  Table 2 (p. 488), and they quote Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. (2008:796) directly. So the
  borrowing is openly declared, not silent.

- **Dille & Söderlund openly reframe temporal fit/misfit in institutional terms.**
  The extraction shows their Table 2 definitions attach the concept to Pérez-Nordtvedt
  while adding the institutional qualifier — fit "due to institutional matching,"
  misfit "due to diverging institutional requirements" (p. 488). This is an
  acknowledged re-casting: they keep the phase/tempo core and explicitly graft on the
  institutional condition.

- **Each paper openly frames the concept's role differently** (self-declared). The
  extraction records Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. presenting entrainment as their headline,
  organization-level model, whereas Dille & Söderlund present it as a supporting
  import beneath their own three core concepts (isochronism/timing norms/temporal
  fit-misfit). Both statements are on the record.

### B.2 — Differences an outside reader would see (neither paper names)

- **One paper defines "entrainment"; the other never does.** Pérez-Nordtvedt et al.
  define the umbrella term explicitly and repeatedly; Dille & Söderlund, per the
  extraction, give no standalone definition and gloss only the sub-types — and source
  those sub-types from **Khavul et al. (2010)**, not from Pérez-Nordtvedt, even while
  crediting Pérez-Nordtvedt with "entrainment theory." Neither paper flags this
  split sourcing.

- **The referent of "temporal fit" shifts.** In Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. fit is
  **organization–environment** (O-E) synchronization — organization to its external
  environment/zeitgeber. In Dille & Söderlund fit is alignment "among the actors
  involved" / "institutional matching" — i.e., **participant-to-participant across a
  project**. Neither paper notes that the concept's two anchor points have changed.

- **Fit as graded vs. fit as binary.** Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. give fit a "tolerance
  range" with satisficing degrees (p. 792); Dille & Söderlund's Table 2 states
  fit/misfit as aligned-vs-diverging institutional conditions, with no tolerance range
  carried over. Neither paper remarks on the loss of the graded structure.

- **Agency vs. homogenization.** Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. treat entrainment as a
  strategic-choice process (entrain vs. temporal enactment; organizations can become
  zeitgebers). Dille & Söderlund deploy it inside **isochronism**, an institutional
  *homogenization* process toward legitimacy. The extraction shows both emphases, but
  neither paper contrasts its stance with the other's.

- **Depth of origin.** Pérez-Nordtvedt et al. supply a full physics→biology→social-
  science lineage; Dille & Söderlund treat Pérez-Nordtvedt (and Khavul et al.) as the
  origin, with no upstream lineage. An outside reader sees the later paper flattening
  the concept's history; neither paper names this.

## C) Open questions for synthesis

*(Surfaced, not resolved. "Incompatible" = the two would answer the same question
differently; "different emphasis" = compatible content, different weighting.)*

**Points that look genuinely incompatible (as worded in the extraction):**

1. **What "temporal fit" is a relation *between*.** Pérez-Nordtvedt et al.: organization
   ↔ external environment/zeitgeber (O-E fit). Dille & Söderlund: actor ↔ actor within a
   project, via "institutional matching." A single case could be in "fit" on one
   definition and "misfit" on the other, because they measure alignment across
   different pairs of things. This is a definitional divergence, not just emphasis.

2. **Structure of the fit construct — graded vs. binary.** A tolerance range with a
   continuum of satisficing performance (Pérez-Nordtvedt) versus an aligned/diverging
   institutional condition (Dille & Söderlund, Table 2). These give structurally
   different answers to "how much misfit is misfit?"

3. **Whether there is a shared definition of "entrainment" to reconcile at all.**
   Pérez-Nordtvedt define the umbrella term; Dille & Söderlund do not, and their
   sub-type definitions come from a different source (Khavul et al. 2010). One cannot
   fully align two definitions when one paper supplies none for the head term.

**Points that look like differences in emphasis only:**

4. **Agency vs. homogenization.** Strategic choice/enactment (Pérez-Nordtvedt) vs.
   institutional homogenization/legitimacy (Dille & Söderlund). Both admit institutional
   forces and some choice; the difference is which is foregrounded.

5. **Unit/level of application.** Organization-level model (Pérez-Nordtvedt) vs.
   project-level institutional framework (Dille & Söderlund). Different site of
   application, not a conflict about what the concept asserts.

6. **Depth of stated origin.** Full lineage vs. second-hand sourcing — a difference in
   how much history each cites, not a disagreement about the concept's content.

**Shared ground worth noting** (from the extraction): both papers build fit/misfit on
the same **tempo + phase** machinery — synchronization of the speed and the timing of
activity cycles. The incompatibilities above sit on top of this common vocabulary,
not underneath it.
